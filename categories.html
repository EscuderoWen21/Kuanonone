Chicken Categories

Organize chickens according to age

4-Month-Old Chickens (Teenagers/Pullets)

  • Still growing and developing; feathers are mostly fully grown in.
  • Should be on grower feed (about 16-18% protein), not layer feed yet.
  • Combs and wattles start to redden and enlarge as they near maturity.
  • Roosters begin crowing and may start sparring; hens are usually not laying yet.
  • Can live outdoors full-time if fully feathered.
View 4-Month-Old Chickens

5-Month-Old Chickens (Nearly Mature)

  • Many hens begin laying their first small eggs (some breeds start as early as 18 weeks, others wait until 6+ months).
  • Switch to layer feed (16% protein with added calcium) around the first egg or at about 18-20 weeks. Offer oyster shell separately.
  • Add nesting boxes now so hens learn where to lay.
  • Pecking order is settling; watch for bullying.
  • Roosters are clearly identifiable by larger combs, long saddle and hackle feathers, and spurs starting to develop.
View 5-Month-Old Chickens

6-Month-Old Chickens and Older (Adults)

  • Fully mature and in regular laying (most hens lay an egg about every 24-26 hours, around 4-6 eggs per week for productive breeds).
  • Eggs reach full size by around 7-8 months.
  • Need layer feed, grit, fresh water, and calcium supplements daily.
  • Roosters are fully mature, fertile, and protective of the flock.
  • Peak laying lasts roughly 1-2 years; production gradually declines each year after that, and hens go through an annual molt, typically in fall, during which laying pauses.
  • With good care, hens can live 5-8+ years.
